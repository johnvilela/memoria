# Session: ai-memory research — why their handoff is faster

**2026-07-29 11:16–11:58 · project memoria · claude-code**

Research-only request: evaluate Fabio Akita's ai-memory on GitHub, explain how its workstream process works, compare its agent handoff/handon with memoria's, and explain why theirs is "so much fast and natural than ours". Explicit constraint: "Do not change anything yet, this is just a research."

Work observed in the digest: one grep over `cmd/memoria/run.go` for the handoff/digest path, then a full report at stop. Sources cited in the report: the [ai-memory repo](https://github.com/akitaonrails/ai-memory) and its `docs/managed-workstreams.md`. The durable comparison is distilled into [[concepts/handoff-vs-ai-memory]].

Headline findings from the report:

- ai-memory's unit of continuity is the **workstream** — persistent, cross-session, cross-harness; memoria's is the session digest file.
- Their cross-harness handoff is **deterministic and LLM-free**: portable history extracted read-only from the prior harness's native transcript store and injected at the SessionStart hook before the first turn. memoria's is a one-sentence "read the session digest at `<path>`" prompt (`run.go:111`), and a high-quality digest needs a detached LLM pass that takes minutes.
- Three root causes for the speed gap: zero-LLM handoff path, push-not-pull context injection, and capture fidelity (their complete native transcripts vs memoria's lossy `@hook` one-liners).
- Stealable ideas recorded for later: build handoff packets from native transcript stores, inject via SessionStart hook, add a workstream-style link id to `sessions.md`.

No files changed; the session ended after delivering the report.
